This is the first post on my personal blog. I decided to create this space to share my thoughts and opinions on different topics, just as they come to mind... without having AI edit or polish them.

## The Technical Barrier vs. Creativity

Part of the reason I wanted to start with this topic is that, thanks to the reduction of this technical barrier, I can now spend more of my time and mental energy on creativity rather than purely technical challenges.

While I believe that both creativity and technical knowledge go hand in hand, mastering the technical side often comes with a steep learning curve. As a result, turning ideas into reality has traditionally required a considerable investment of time and effort just to learn the necessary skills.

I'm not trying to diminish the value of learning things the traditional way. There is still great value in understanding how things work. However, using AI allows us to focus our time and energy on the things we actually want to learn and build, instead of getting blocked by every technical obstacle along the way.

The arrival of the first practical AI systems marks a clear before and after in human history. I can only imagine what the world will look like once we achieve Artificial General Intelligence (AGI).

## The Countdown Has Begun

Ever since OpenAI released the first widely adopted GPT model to the public, the progress of these models has been nothing short of exponential. Very few people were truly prepared for what was coming.

Every new frontier model that is released removes another piece of the technical barrier that exists across so many fields of human knowledge. We're getting closer to the point where AI systems will be capable of improving themselves, and that will be a defining moment.

The era of technical barriers is coming to an end, and it's time to place even greater value on the creative side of being human—that abstract ability we have to imagine, create, and appreciate new ideas.

## Change Is Painful

Looking through social media, I've noticed a growing resentment toward AI from many purists, experts, and professionals who spent years mastering their craft.

In many ways, the exclusivity of those skills has disappeared. Capabilities that once required years of study are now accessible to people who never had to go through the same struggle.

I understand why that feels frustrating. But life isn't fair, and it isn't unfair either. It simply is.

The only real option is to adapt. Fortunately, adapting is something humans have always been remarkably good at—hopefully, good enough this time as well.
